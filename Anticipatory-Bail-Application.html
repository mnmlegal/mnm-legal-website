Anticipatory Bail in India: Section 482 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023

Understanding pre-arrest protection, eligibility, statutory restrictions, judicial discretion, court procedure and the constitutional importance of personal liberty.

MNM Legal Associates Criminal Law Legal Guide

Personal liberty is a fundamental constitutional protection under Article 21 of the Constitution of India.

Anticipatory bail is a statutory remedy available to a person who reasonably apprehends arrest on an accusation of having committed a non-bailable offence.

The remedy seeks to balance the protection of individual liberty with the legitimate requirements of criminal investigation and administration of justice.

Current Statutory Provision

Section 482 BNSS

Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023.

Former Statutory Provision

Section 438 CrPC

Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, where applicable under the savings provisions.

1. What Is Anticipatory Bail?

Anticipatory bail is commonly understood as pre-arrest bail. It is a direction issued by a competent court that, in the event of arrest in connection with the accusation covered by the order, the applicant shall be released on bail.

Unlike regular bail, which ordinarily concerns a person already arrested or in custody, anticipatory bail is sought before arrest.

Anticipatory Bail Does Not Terminate Investigation

An anticipatory bail order does not quash an FIR, terminate criminal proceedings or establish the innocence of the applicant. Investigation may continue in accordance with law and the conditions imposed by the court.

2. Statutory Framework Under Section 482 BNSS

The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 came into force on 1 July 2024, replacing the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, subject to applicable transitional and savings provisions.

Section 482 BNSS empowers the High Court or Court of Session to grant a direction for release on bail in the event of arrest where a person has reason to believe that they may be arrested on an accusation of having committed a non-bailable offence.

The corresponding provision under the former Code of Criminal Procedure was Section 438.

Judicial precedents interpreting Section 438 CrPC continue to be relevant to the extent that their principles remain consistent with the current statutory framework.

Applicability of the Former CrPC

Section 531 BNSS contains repeal and savings provisions. The applicable procedural law must be determined with reference to the relevant proceedings, their commencement and the statutory transitional requirements.

3. Who Can Apply for Anticipatory Bail?

A person who has reasonable grounds to apprehend arrest on an accusation of having committed a non-bailable offence may approach the competent court, subject to applicable statutory restrictions.

The apprehension of arrest must be based on identifiable facts and circumstances. A vague or purely speculative apprehension may not be sufficient.

Application Before Registration of FIR

Registration of an FIR is not invariably a prerequisite for seeking anticipatory bail.

Where a reasonable apprehension of arrest exists in relation to an identifiable accusation, an application may be maintainable even before registration of the FIR.

Anticipatory bail cannot ordinarily be granted as a blanket protection against unspecified future criminal accusations.

4. Which Court Can Grant Anticipatory Bail?

Section 482 BNSS confers jurisdiction upon the following courts:

  • The Court of Session.
  • The High Court.

Both courts possess statutory jurisdiction to consider anticipatory bail applications.

Ordinarily, an applicant may approach the competent Sessions Court in the first instance, subject to applicable judicial practice, territorial jurisdiction and the circumstances of the case.

Where appropriate, the High Court may also be approached in accordance with law.

Anticipatory Bail Proceedings in Ahmedabad

For criminal matters arising within Ahmedabad, the competent Sessions Court must be identified with reference to the territorial jurisdiction of the concerned police station, the alleged offence and applicable court allocation.

The High Court of Gujarat may exercise its jurisdiction under Section 482 BNSS in accordance with applicable law.

Transit Anticipatory Bail

In appropriate circumstances, a court outside the territorial jurisdiction of the registered FIR may consider granting limited transit protection to enable an applicant to approach the jurisdictional court.

Such relief is exceptional and subject to safeguards, territorial considerations and the principles laid down by the Supreme Court.

5. Grounds Considered by the Court

Grant of anticipatory bail is a matter of judicial discretion. The court considers the relevant facts, allegations and circumstances of each case.

Relevant considerations may include:

  • Nature and gravity of the alleged offence.
  • Specific role attributed to the applicant.
  • Available material supporting the allegations.
  • Previous criminal antecedents.
  • Possibility of the applicant absconding or evading justice.
  • Necessity of custodial interrogation.
  • Possibility of tampering with evidence.
  • Possibility of influencing or intimidating witnesses.
  • Willingness to cooperate with the investigation.
  • Allegations of false implication, mala fide intention or abuse of criminal process, where supported by material.
  • Other circumstances relevant to the administration of justice.

Requirement of Custodial Interrogation

The necessity of custodial interrogation may be a significant consideration while deciding an application for anticipatory bail.

However, the absence of a requirement for custodial interrogation does not, by itself, create an absolute entitlement to anticipatory bail.

The court must assess the overall circumstances and competing interests involved.

6. Statutory Restrictions on Anticipatory Bail

Anticipatory bail is not available without restriction in every category of criminal offence.

Section 482(4) BNSS

Section 482(4) provides that the anticipatory bail provision shall not apply to cases involving accusations of offences under:

  • Section 65 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023.
  • Section 70(2) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023.

These provisions concern specified serious sexual offences involving victims below the prescribed ages.

Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989

Sections 18 and 18A of the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act impose restrictions on the grant of anticipatory bail.

The Supreme Court has recognised that the statutory bar does not apply where the allegations do not disclose a prima facie offence under the Act.

The court's examination at this stage is subject to the limited scope recognised by applicable judicial precedents.

Special Criminal Statutes

Special statutes may impose additional restrictions, special bail conditions or an express exclusion of anticipatory bail.

For example, Section 43D(4) of the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967 excludes the application of anticipatory bail provisions in cases involving offences punishable under that Act.

The Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985 and the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 may impose stringent bail requirements in cases falling within their respective statutory provisions.

Special Laws Require Separate Examination

The maintainability of an anticipatory bail application must be assessed with reference to the precise offences, applicable special statute and relevant judicial interpretation.

7. Anticipatory Bail Application Procedure

  1. Examination of Allegations The relevant FIR, complaint, statutory provisions and surrounding circumstances are examined to determine the nature of the accusation and apprehension of arrest.
  2. Determination of Jurisdiction The appropriate Sessions Court or High Court is identified in accordance with applicable law.
  3. Preparation of Application The application sets out the relevant facts, grounds for relief, criminal antecedents, where applicable, and supporting documents.
  4. Filing Before Competent Court The application is filed in accordance with the applicable court rules and procedural requirements.
  5. Consideration of Interim Relief Where justified by the circumstances, interim protection may be requested pending final consideration of the application.
  6. Response of Prosecution The prosecution is afforded an opportunity to respond in accordance with the applicable procedure and directions of the court.
  7. Hearing and Judicial Order The court considers the submissions, relevant material and statutory requirements before deciding the application.
  8. Compliance With Bail Conditions If anticipatory bail is granted, the applicant must comply with the conditions and directions contained in the order.

8. Interim Protection From Arrest

Pending final determination of an anticipatory bail application, the competent court may consider granting interim protection in appropriate circumstances.

Interim relief is discretionary and depends upon the facts, applicable law and the requirements of justice.

Filing an Application Does Not Automatically Prevent Arrest

Mere filing or pendency of an anticipatory bail application does not, by itself, operate as a stay on arrest. Protection depends upon the existence and terms of an applicable judicial order or other legal safeguard.

9. Conditions Under Section 482(2) BNSS

While granting anticipatory bail, the High Court or Court of Session may impose conditions appropriate to the facts of the case.

Section 482(2) recognises conditions including:

  • Making oneself available for interrogation by a police officer as and when required.
  • Not directly or indirectly making any inducement, threat or promise to a person acquainted with the facts of the case so as to dissuade that person from disclosing such facts to the court or police.
  • Not leaving India without prior permission of the court.
  • Other conditions that may be imposed under Section 480(3) BNSS, as applicable.

Depending upon the circumstances, the court may also impose lawful conditions concerning appearance, cooperation, bonds, sureties and other matters necessary to secure the administration of justice.

Bail conditions must be legally sustainable and reasonably connected with the purpose for which they are imposed.

10. Duration of Anticipatory Bail

Anticipatory bail need not ordinarily be restricted to a fixed period.

In Sushila Aggarwal v. State (NCT of Delhi), (2020) 5 SCC 1, the Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court held that protection granted under anticipatory bail need not invariably be limited to a fixed period and may continue until the conclusion of trial.

Depending upon the special facts and circumstances, the court may impose appropriate limitations or conditions.

The precise scope and duration of protection must therefore be determined from the judicial order and applicable law.

11. What Happens if Anticipatory Bail Is Rejected?

Rejection of an anticipatory bail application does not amount to a finding of guilt.

Where an application is rejected by the Sessions Court, the applicant may approach the High Court in accordance with law.

Previous applications, orders and relevant proceedings must be appropriately disclosed.

If relief is declined by the High Court, further remedies may be considered in accordance with the applicable constitutional and procedural framework.

Where an applicant is arrested or surrenders into custody, regular bail may be sought subject to the applicable provisions.

Successive Applications

Repeated anticipatory bail applications cannot ordinarily be used to circumvent earlier judicial orders. Maintainability may depend upon a genuine change in circumstances and applicable judicial principles.

12. Cancellation of Anticipatory Bail

Anticipatory bail may be cancelled or an improper bail order may be set aside by a competent court in accordance with law.

Relevant circumstances may include:

  • Violation of material bail conditions.
  • Interference with the investigation.
  • Tampering with evidence.
  • Intimidation or unlawful influence upon witnesses.
  • Absconding or evasion of judicial proceedings.
  • Material suppression or misrepresentation before the court.
  • Other legally recognised grounds for cancellation or setting aside of bail.

Cancellation of bail on account of subsequent misconduct is conceptually distinct from setting aside a bail order that was legally unsustainable when granted.

13. Anticipatory Bail, Regular Bail and Default Bail

The appropriate bail remedy depends upon the nature of the offence, stage of proceedings, custody status and applicable statutory provisions.

14. Documents Commonly Required

Documentary requirements may vary depending upon the facts and circumstances of the criminal case.

  • Copy of the FIR, where available.
  • Copy of the complaint or relevant accusation.
  • Police notices or communications, if any.
  • Identity and address documents, where required.
  • Relevant documentary evidence supporting the applicant's case.
  • Previous judicial orders concerning the matter.
  • Details of previous bail applications.
  • Relevant medical records, where applicable.
  • Affidavit, vakalatnama and other documents required under the applicable court rules.

All relevant facts and previous proceedings should be accurately disclosed before the court.

15. Landmark Supreme Court Judgments

The following judicial authorities explain important principles governing anticipatory bail and protection of personal liberty.

Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia v. State of Punjab

(1980) 2 SCC 565

The Constitution Bench explained the scope of anticipatory bail and emphasised that judicial discretion should be exercised with reference to the circumstances of the individual case, rather than rigid, inflexible limitations.

Siddharam Satlingappa Mhetre v. State of Maharashtra

(2011) 1 SCC 694

The Supreme Court discussed the constitutional importance of personal liberty and relevant considerations governing the exercise of anticipatory bail jurisdiction.

Sushila Aggarwal v. State (NCT of Delhi)

(2020) 5 SCC 1

The Constitution Bench held that anticipatory bail need not ordinarily be limited to a fixed duration and may continue until conclusion of trial, subject to appropriate conditions and exceptional circumstances.

Prathvi Raj Chauhan v. Union of India

(2020) 4 SCC 727

The Supreme Court considered the statutory restrictions on anticipatory bail under the SC/ST Act and recognised that the bar does not apply where a prima facie offence under the Act is not disclosed.

State represented by CBI v. Anil Sharma

(1997) 7 SCC 187

The Supreme Court considered the significance of custodial interrogation while examining an application for anticipatory bail.

Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar

(2014) 8 SCC 273

The Supreme Court issued directions concerning unnecessary arrests and compliance with statutory safeguards governing the exercise of police powers of arrest.

Priya Indoria v. State of Karnataka

2023 SCC OnLine SC 1484

The Supreme Court examined the power of courts to grant limited transit anticipatory bail in appropriate circumstances to facilitate access to the jurisdictional court.

Application of Judicial Precedents

Judicial decisions must be read in their factual and statutory context. Earlier precedents under the CrPC remain relevant insofar as their principles are consistent with the applicable BNSS provisions and subsequent judicial developments.

16. Frequently Asked Questions

Can anticipatory bail be obtained before registration of an FIR?

Yes, in appropriate circumstances. A reasonable apprehension of arrest concerning an identifiable accusation must exist.

Which section provides for anticipatory bail under BNSS?

Section 482 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 provides for anticipatory bail, subject to applicable statutory restrictions.

Is anticipatory bail available after arrest?

Anticipatory bail is a pre-arrest remedy. Once a person has been arrested in the concerned case, regular bail is ordinarily the appropriate remedy.

Does filing an anticipatory bail application prevent arrest?

No. Mere filing does not automatically prevent arrest. Protection depends upon applicable judicial orders and statutory safeguards.

Can the High Court be approached directly for anticipatory bail?

Section 482 confers jurisdiction upon both the High Court and Court of Session. Applicable judicial practice and the circumstances of the case must be considered.

Is anticipatory bail permanent?

Anticipatory bail need not ordinarily be time-limited and may continue until conclusion of trial. Its duration and scope depend upon the judicial order and applicable law.

Can police continue investigation after anticipatory bail is granted?

Yes. Anticipatory bail does not prevent lawful investigation. The applicant must comply with the conditions imposed by the court.

Can anticipatory bail be cancelled?

Yes. A competent court may cancel bail or set aside an improper bail order on legally recognised grounds.

Is anticipatory bail available for every non-bailable offence?

No. Section 482(4) BNSS and certain special statutes impose restrictions or exclusions. The precise statutory provisions must be examined.

Can anticipatory bail be granted in matrimonial criminal disputes?

An application may be maintainable in appropriate matrimonial criminal disputes, subject to the alleged offences, statutory restrictions and circumstances of the case.

How long does an anticipatory bail application take?

There is no uniform disposal period applicable to every case. Urgency, court allocation, prosecution response and other procedural circumstances may affect the hearing and disposal of the application.

Does anticipatory bail mean that the FIR has been quashed?

No. Anticipatory bail and quashing of an FIR are distinct legal remedies. Grant of bail does not terminate criminal proceedings.

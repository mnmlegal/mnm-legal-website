Maintenance Law in India: A Comprehensive Legal Guide

Understanding the legal right to financial support, interim maintenance, permanent alimony, child support and the statutory remedies available under Indian law.

MNM Legal Associates Family Law Legal Guide

Maintenance is a statutory financial remedy intended to provide support to persons who are legally entitled to receive assistance from another person.

Indian law recognises maintenance obligations in several circumstances, including matrimonial disputes, separation, divorce, domestic violence, child support and the maintenance of parents.

Entitlement to maintenance, the amount payable and the procedure for obtaining relief depend upon the applicable statutory provision and the individual circumstances of the parties.

1. What Is Maintenance?

Maintenance refers to financial support provided to a person who is entitled to receive such support under applicable law.

Depending upon the governing provision, maintenance may include reasonable expenses relating to food, clothing, residence, education, medical treatment and other necessary living requirements.

Maintenance is not necessarily dependent upon the dissolution of marriage. An eligible person may seek maintenance during marriage, separation, pending matrimonial proceedings or after divorce, subject to applicable law.

Who May Claim Maintenance?

  • A wife who satisfies the applicable statutory requirements.
  • A divorced wife who remains eligible under the relevant provision.
  • A husband under specified matrimonial statutes.
  • Eligible children.
  • Parents who satisfy the statutory conditions.
  • Other eligible dependants under applicable personal or statutory law.

2. Statutory Framework Governing Maintenance

Maintenance remedies are available under several Indian statutes. Each provision has its own scope, eligibility requirements and procedural framework.

Statutory Provision Nature of Remedy
Section 144, BNSS, 2023 Maintenance for eligible wives, children and parents.
Section 24, Hindu Marriage Act Interim maintenance and litigation expenses for either eligible spouse.
Section 25, Hindu Marriage Act Permanent alimony and maintenance.
Section 26, Hindu Marriage Act Custody, maintenance and education of minor children.
Section 20, Domestic Violence Act Monetary relief, including maintenance for eligible aggrieved women and children.
Sections 18–20, HAMA, 1956 Maintenance rights of wives, eligible widowed daughters-in-law, children and aged or infirm parents, subject to statutory conditions.
Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007 Maintenance and welfare remedies for eligible parents and senior citizens.
Important Legal Distinction

Different maintenance remedies may coexist. However, the existence of multiple statutory remedies does not automatically entitle a claimant to duplicate recovery for the same period and purpose.

3. Maintenance Under Section 144 BNSS

Section 144 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 provides a statutory remedy where a person having sufficient means neglects or refuses to maintain specified persons entitled to support.

This provision broadly corresponds to Section 125 of the former Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973. The BNSS came into force on 1 July 2024, subject to applicable commencement and transitional provisions.

Maintenance of Wife

A wife who is unable to maintain herself may seek maintenance where her husband, despite having sufficient means, neglects or refuses to maintain her.

The statutory definition of wife includes a woman who has been divorced by, or has obtained a divorce from, her husband and has not remarried.

Maintenance of Children

Section 144 provides maintenance remedies for legitimate and illegitimate minor children who are unable to maintain themselves, subject to the statutory requirements.

An adult child, other than a married daughter, may also qualify under specified circumstances involving physical or mental abnormality or injury resulting in an inability to maintain themselves.

Maintenance of Parents

A father or mother who is unable to maintain themselves may seek maintenance from a child having sufficient means, subject to the requirements of the provision.

Statutory Restrictions

Section 144(4) specifies circumstances in which a wife may not be entitled to maintenance under that provision, including:

  • Living in adultery.
  • Refusal to live with her husband without sufficient reason.
  • Living separately by mutual consent.

The application of these restrictions depends upon the facts, evidence, marital status and applicable judicial interpretation. A mere allegation does not establish a statutory disqualification.

Maintenance Is Not Automatic

The competent court must consider statutory eligibility, sufficient means, neglect or refusal, the financial circumstances of the parties and any applicable defences.

4. Interim Maintenance and Litigation Expenses

Interim maintenance is temporary financial assistance granted during the pendency of legal proceedings.

Its purpose is to address immediate financial needs while the principal dispute remains pending before the court.

Section 24 of the Hindu Marriage Act

Section 24 provides for maintenance pendente lite and expenses of proceedings where either the wife or the husband has no independent income sufficient for their support and necessary litigation expenses.

The court may direct the other spouse to pay reasonable litigation expenses and a monthly amount during the proceedings, having regard to the income of both parties.

Can a Husband Claim Interim Maintenance?

Yes. Section 24 of the Hindu Marriage Act is available to either spouse, subject to the statutory requirements and judicial assessment of their financial circumstances.

However, this does not mean that a husband can claim spousal maintenance under every maintenance provision. Section 144 BNSS, for example, does not provide a corresponding remedy to a husband in his capacity as a spouse.

5. Permanent Alimony and Maintenance

Section 25 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 empowers the court, at the time of passing a decree or subsequently, to grant permanent alimony and maintenance to either eligible spouse.

Depending upon the circumstances, the court may direct payment of:

  • A gross or lump-sum amount.
  • Monthly payments.
  • Other periodic payments for a term not exceeding the life of the applicant.

While determining permanent alimony, the court may consider the income and property of both parties, their conduct and other relevant circumstances.

An order for permanent alimony may also be varied, modified or rescinded in circumstances recognised under Section 25.

6. Maintenance Under the Domestic Violence Act

The Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 provides several statutory remedies to eligible aggrieved women.

Section 20 — Monetary Relief

Under Section 20, the Magistrate may direct payment of monetary relief to meet expenses incurred and losses suffered as a result of domestic violence.

Such relief may include:

  • Loss of earnings.
  • Medical expenses.
  • Loss caused by destruction, damage or removal of property.
  • Maintenance for the aggrieved woman and her children.

Monetary relief must be adequate, fair and reasonable and consistent with the standard of living to which the aggrieved person is accustomed.

Section 23 — Interim and Ex Parte Orders

Section 23 empowers the Magistrate to grant appropriate interim orders and, where statutory requirements are satisfied, ex parte orders.

Maintenance under the Domestic Violence Act may be considered alongside other existing maintenance proceedings and orders, subject to disclosure and appropriate adjustment.

7. Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956

The Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956 provides substantive maintenance rights to specified persons within its statutory scope.

Section 18 — Maintenance of Wife

Section 18 recognises the right of a Hindu wife to be maintained by her husband during her lifetime, subject to the provisions of the Act.

The provision also recognises specified circumstances in which a wife may live separately without forfeiting her right to maintenance.

Section 19 — Maintenance of Widowed Daughter-in-Law

A Hindu widowed daughter-in-law may claim maintenance from her father-in-law in circumstances specified under Section 19, subject to statutory conditions concerning her own means, available sources of support and relevant coparcenary property.

Section 20 — Maintenance of Children and Parents

Section 20 recognises maintenance obligations towards children and aged or infirm parents.

The obligation may extend to an unmarried daughter who is unable to maintain herself out of her own earnings or property, subject to the applicable statutory conditions.

8. How Is Maintenance Calculated?

Indian maintenance law does not prescribe a universal fixed percentage of salary applicable to every case.

The amount is determined through a judicial assessment of the financial circumstances, reasonable requirements and obligations of the parties.

Relevant Considerations

  • Actual income and financial resources of both parties.
  • Employment, qualifications and relevant earning capacity.
  • Reasonable needs of the claimant.
  • Standard of living during the marriage.
  • Assets, investments and property.
  • Genuine liabilities and financial obligations.
  • Educational and medical expenses of children.
  • Responsibilities towards dependent family members.
  • Age, health and other relevant personal circumstances.
Is Maintenance Always 25% of Salary?

No. Judicial observations concerning particular percentages in individual cases do not create a universal statutory formula. Maintenance must be assessed according to the facts and applicable law in each case.

9. Supreme Court Guidelines in Rajnesh v. Neha

In Rajnesh v. Neha, (2021) 2 SCC 324, the Supreme Court of India issued significant guidelines concerning maintenance proceedings.

The judgment addresses recurring issues involving financial disclosure, overlapping maintenance jurisdiction, determination of quantum, the date from which maintenance should be awarded and enforcement of maintenance orders.

Disclosure of Assets and Liabilities

The parties are required to furnish financial disclosure affidavits in accordance with the applicable guidelines and prescribed formats.

Such disclosures assist the court in assessing income, assets, expenditure, liabilities and the financial position of the parties.

Overlapping Maintenance Proceedings

Previous maintenance proceedings and orders must be disclosed in subsequent maintenance proceedings.

The court considering a subsequent claim must take existing awards into account and consider appropriate adjustment or set-off.

Date From Which Maintenance Is Awarded

The Supreme Court directed that maintenance should be awarded from the date of filing the maintenance application.

The applicable statutory provisions and binding judicial directions must be considered while determining the effective date of a maintenance order.

Employment of the Claimant

The mere fact that a spouse is educated or employed does not necessarily defeat a maintenance claim.

The court must assess whether the actual income is sufficient, having regard to the applicable legal requirements and circumstances.

10. General Procedure for Claiming Maintenance

  1. Identification of Applicable Remedy Determine the appropriate statutory provision, eligibility requirements and competent forum.
  2. Preparation of Application Prepare the application containing relevant facts, financial circumstances, statutory grounds and reliefs sought.
  3. Filing Before the Competent Court Present the application before the appropriate court or authority in accordance with jurisdictional and procedural requirements.
  4. Notice and Response The opposite party is ordinarily given an opportunity to appear and respond to the claim.
  5. Financial Disclosure The parties submit applicable affidavits of assets and liabilities and supporting financial records.
  6. Interim Relief Where legally available and appropriate, the court may determine interim maintenance during the pendency of proceedings.
  7. Evidence and Final Determination The court considers the pleadings, evidence, financial disclosures and legal submissions before passing an appropriate order.

In Ahmedabad, the appropriate Family Court, Magistrate or other competent authority must be identified according to the applicable statute and territorial jurisdiction.

11. Documents Commonly Required

The documentary requirements depend upon the nature of the claim and applicable procedural rules.

  • Identity and address proof.
  • Marriage certificate or other evidence of marriage, where relevant.
  • Salary slips and employment records.
  • Bank account statements.
  • Income tax returns and relevant financial records.
  • Business income records, where applicable.
  • Details of movable and immovable assets.
  • Loan statements and other liabilities.
  • Children's educational and medical expenditure records.
  • Rent, household and other reasonable expenditure records.
  • Existing maintenance orders and related court proceedings.
  • Applicable affidavit of assets and liabilities.

Financial information submitted before the court should be accurate, complete and supported by relevant documents wherever reasonably available.

12. Modification or Cancellation of Maintenance

Maintenance orders may be subject to alteration where a material change in circumstances is established and the applicable statutory requirements are satisfied.

Section 146 BNSS

Section 146 provides for alteration of maintenance or interim maintenance allowances upon proof of a change in circumstances, along with other specified grounds for variation or cancellation.

Section 25(2), Hindu Marriage Act

Where there is a change in the circumstances of either party after an order for permanent alimony, the court may vary, modify or rescind the order as it considers just, subject to the provision.

Relevant Changes May Include

  • Substantial increase or reduction in income.
  • Genuine loss of employment.
  • Material changes in medical or educational expenditure.
  • Changes in financial dependency.
  • Remarriage, where relevant under the applicable statutory provision.
Existing Orders Remain Operative

A person should not assume that a change in financial circumstances automatically cancels an existing maintenance order. Appropriate modification, cancellation or stay must be sought where legally required.

13. Enforcement of Maintenance Orders

Where a person fails to comply with a maintenance order, the beneficiary may seek enforcement through the procedure prescribed by the applicable law.

Enforcement Under BNSS

Section 144(3) BNSS provides for recovery proceedings where a person fails without sufficient cause to comply with a maintenance order.

Subject to statutory conditions, the Magistrate may issue a warrant for recovery and may impose imprisonment in accordance with the provision.

An application for issuance of a recovery warrant under Section 144(3) is subject to the statutory requirement that it be made within one year from the date on which the relevant amount became due.

Section 147 BNSS also addresses enforcement of maintenance orders in accordance with its terms.

Enforcement of Matrimonial Court Orders

Orders and decrees under the Hindu Marriage Act may be enforced in accordance with Section 28A and applicable civil execution procedures.

The appropriate enforcement remedy depends upon the statute under which the original order was passed.

14. Frequently Asked Questions

Can maintenance be claimed without divorce?

Yes. Eligible persons may claim maintenance during marriage or separation under applicable statutory provisions without first obtaining a divorce.

Can a working wife claim maintenance?

Employment does not automatically disqualify a wife. The court considers her actual income, reasonable needs and other relevant statutory circumstances.

Can a husband claim maintenance from his wife?

Sections 24 and 25 of the Hindu Marriage Act provide remedies to either eligible spouse, subject to their respective statutory requirements.

Is maintenance always 25% of salary?

No. There is no universal statutory percentage. The amount depends upon the financial circumstances and applicable legal principles.

Can maintenance be increased or reduced later?

Yes. A competent court may modify a maintenance order upon proof of a relevant change in circumstances, subject to the applicable statutory provisions.

Can maintenance be claimed after divorce?

Yes, subject to statutory eligibility, existing orders, settlement terms, remarriage and other relevant circumstances.

Can maintenance be claimed under multiple laws?

Multiple statutory remedies may be available. Existing proceedings and awards must be disclosed, and the court must consider appropriate adjustment to avoid duplicate recovery.

Does unemployment automatically exempt a person from paying maintenance?

No. The court may examine the person's actual financial resources, earning capacity, reasons for unemployment and other relevant circumstances.

Can parents claim maintenance from their children?

Yes. Section 144 BNSS and other applicable statutes provide maintenance remedies to eligible parents, subject to statutory conditions.

What happens if maintenance is not paid?

The beneficiary may initiate appropriate enforcement proceedings under the statute governing the maintenance order, subject to applicable conditions and limitation requirements.

How long does a maintenance case take?

The duration depends upon service of notice, financial disclosures, interim applications, evidence, court workload and other procedural circumstances. A uniform disposal period cannot be guaranteed.

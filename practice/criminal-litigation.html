Criminal Law and Judicial Remedies

Criminal litigation involves proceedings concerning alleged offences, investigation, prosecution, defence and determination of criminal liability before competent judicial authorities.

MNM Legal Associates, based in Chandkheda, Ahmedabad, deals with criminal law matters involving bail proceedings, criminal complaints, FIR-related remedies, trial proceedings, criminal appeals and revisions.

Criminal proceedings require careful examination of the allegations, available evidence, procedural safeguards, applicable statutory provisions and relevant judicial precedents.

Each matter must be assessed independently, with due regard to professional confidentiality, the rights of the parties and the requirements of the administration of justice.

Principal Criminal Laws

Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023

Substantive criminal offences, punishments and criminal liability.

Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023

Criminal procedure, investigation, arrest, bail, trial and judicial remedies.

Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023

Admissibility, relevancy and proof of evidence, including electronic records.

Special Criminal Enactments

Additional statutory requirements governing specified categories of offences.

Judicial Process

Stages of Criminal Proceedings

The following illustrates a general criminal procedure. The actual sequence varies according to the nature of the offence, applicable law and judicial orders.

01

Complaint or FIR

Initiation of proceedings through information concerning an alleged offence or a complaint before a competent authority.

02

Investigation

Collection of evidence, examination of relevant persons and other lawful investigative procedures.

03

Arrest, Remand & Bail

Consideration of custody, remand and available bail remedies where arrest or apprehension of arrest arises.

04

Police Report & Cognizance

Submission of the police report and consideration of further proceedings by the competent court.

05

Trial & Evidence

Consideration of charges, examination of evidence, cross-examination and submissions of the parties, as applicable.

06

Judgment & Further Remedies

Judicial determination followed by available appellate, revisional or other statutory remedies.

Constitutional Safeguards

Fundamental Rights in Criminal Proceedings

Constitutional protections and statutory safeguards form an essential part of the criminal justice system.

Equality Before Law

Article 14 guarantees equality before the law and equal protection of laws.

Life & Personal Liberty

Article 21 protects life and personal liberty except according to procedure established by law.

Protection Against Self-Incrimination

Article 20(3) protects a person accused of an offence against being compelled to be a witness against themselves.

Safeguards Against Arrest & Detention

Article 22 provides specified protections concerning arrest, detention, access to legal representation and production before a Magistrate.

Right to Speedy Trial

The right to speedy trial has been recognised by the Supreme Court as an essential aspect of Article 21.

Access to Legal Aid

Article 39A and the applicable legal services framework recognise the importance of equal access to justice and free legal aid for eligible persons.

Legal Awareness

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an FIR and a criminal complaint?

An FIR generally refers to information recorded by police under Section 173 BNSS concerning a cognizable offence. A criminal complaint, in its statutory sense, is an allegation made to a Magistrate with a view to action under the BNSS and generally excludes a police report, subject to the statutory explanation.

Does registration of an FIR automatically result in arrest?

No. Arrest must comply with the applicable statutory requirements. Section 35 BNSS provides safeguards and circumstances governing arrest without warrant and notices of appearance.

Can bail be granted before arrest?

Anticipatory bail may be sought under Section 482 BNSS where a person apprehends arrest for a non-bailable offence, subject to statutory exclusions and applicable special enactments.

Can an FIR be quashed by the High Court?

Yes. The High Court may exercise its inherent jurisdiction under Section 528 BNSS in appropriate cases. Quashing is subject to recognised judicial principles and the facts of the particular matter.

Can every criminal case be settled between the parties?

No. Section 359 BNSS specifies offences that may be compounded, including certain offences requiring court permission. Settlement-based quashing of other proceedings is subject to distinct judicial principles and restrictions.

Can electronic records be used as evidence?

Yes. Electronic and digital records may be admissible subject to applicable evidentiary requirements, including Sections 61 to 63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, where applicable.

What remedies are available if police refuse to register an FIR?

In appropriate cases involving cognizable offences, remedies may include a written representation to the Superintendent of Police under Section 173(4) BNSS and an application before the competent Magistrate under Section 175(3), subject to statutory procedural requirements.

Can a criminal conviction be challenged?

A conviction may be challenged through available appellate remedies, subject to statutory maintainability, jurisdiction and limitation requirements.

Does grant of bail mean the criminal case has ended?

No. Bail concerns release from custody. Investigation and criminal proceedings may continue in accordance with law.

Which court has jurisdiction over criminal matters in Ahmedabad?

Jurisdiction depends upon the alleged offence, concerned police station, territorial limits and applicable enactment. Proceedings may fall before a competent Magistrate, Sessions Court, designated Special Court or the High Court of Gujarat, as applicable.

Consumer Rights and Statutory Remedies

The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 establishes a statutory framework for protecting consumer interests and resolving eligible disputes arising from the purchase of goods and provision of services.

Consumer disputes may arise from defective products, deficient services, wrongful rejection of insurance claims, banking irregularities, misleading representations, unfair contractual conditions and other legally recognised grounds.

MNM Legal Associates, based in Chandkheda, Ahmedabad, deals with consumer disputes, legal notices, consumer complaints, written versions, rejoinders, evidence affidavits, appeals and enforcement proceedings.

Each matter requires examination of the underlying consumer relationship, contractual obligations, consideration paid, statutory jurisdiction, limitation and available documentary evidence.

Consumer protection remedies are subject to statutory eligibility and maintainability. Every contractual or commercial dispute does not automatically constitute a consumer dispute.

Statutory Jurisdiction

Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions

Ordinary original consumer complaints are allocated according to the applicable pecuniary jurisdiction rules.

District Commission

Up to ₹50 Lakh

Ordinary original complaints within the prescribed consideration threshold. Section 34 read with the 2021 Jurisdiction Rules.

State Commission

Above ₹50 Lakh to ₹2 Crore

Ordinary original complaints falling within the prescribed monetary range, along with applicable appellate and revisional jurisdiction.

National Commission

Above ₹2 Crore

Ordinary original complaints above the prescribed threshold, together with applicable appellate and revisional jurisdiction.

Important: Ordinary pecuniary jurisdiction is determined by the legally relevant value paid as consideration for goods or services, rather than the aggregate compensation demanded. Special jurisdictional provisions concerning unfair contract complaints, appeals and revisions must be assessed separately.

Official Jurisdiction Rules, 2021 — Gazette Notification G.S.R. 912(E)

Section 2(7)

Who Qualifies as a Consumer?

Purchase of Goods

A person purchasing goods for consideration paid, promised, partly paid and partly promised, or under a deferred payment arrangement, may qualify as a consumer.

The definition also includes certain approved users of such goods, subject to statutory conditions and exclusions.

Hiring or Availing Services

A person hiring or availing services for consideration may qualify as a consumer, together with eligible beneficiaries of such services.

Banking, financing, insurance, transport and housing construction fall within the statutory service framework, subject to applicable law.

Commercial Purpose Exclusion

Transactions involving resale or commercial purposes may fall outside consumer protection jurisdiction.

The statutory exception concerning goods used exclusively to earn a livelihood through self-employment must be considered wherever applicable.

Online & Offline Transactions

The statutory definition encompasses eligible transactions undertaken through traditional and electronic modes, including online purchases, teleshopping and direct selling.

Eligibility remains subject to the nature and purpose of the transaction.

Statutory Grounds

Grounds for Consumer Complaints

Deficiency in Service

Section 2(11) covers legally recognised shortcomings in the quality, nature or manner of performance of services.

  • Failure to perform agreed obligations.
  • Negligent acts or omissions causing loss.
  • Unjustified service deficiencies.
  • Deliberate withholding of relevant information.

Defect in Goods

Section 2(10) concerns shortcomings in the legally required or represented quality, quantity, potency, purity or standard of goods.

  • Manufacturing and quality defects.
  • Non-compliance with promised specifications.
  • Defective performance.
  • Failure to meet applicable standards.

Unfair Trade Practice

Section 2(47) identifies various unfair or deceptive practices used in promoting the supply or use of goods and services.

  • False representations.
  • Misleading advertisements.
  • Deceptive offers and pricing.
  • Other conduct covered by the provision.

Unfair Contract

Section 2(46) addresses specified contractual terms that materially prejudice consumer rights.

  • Manifestly excessive security deposits.
  • Disproportionate penalties.
  • Unreasonable unilateral termination.
  • Other unreasonable consumer obligations.
A consumer complaint may involve multiple statutory grounds. The applicable provisions must be identified from the facts rather than relying upon general allegations of unfairness or contractual breach.
Territorial Jurisdiction

Where Can a Consumer Complaint Be Filed?

Sections 34(2) and 47(4) provide territorial jurisdiction criteria for District and State Commission proceedings.

Residence or Workplace of Complainant

The Act permits institution of eligible complaints where the complainant resides or personally works for gain, subject to the applicable statutory provision.

Location of Opposite Party

The residence, business location, branch office or workplace of the opposite party may establish jurisdiction in accordance with statutory requirements.

Accrual of Cause of Action

A complaint may be instituted where the cause of action arose, either wholly or in part, subject to other jurisdictional requirements.

Multiple Opposite Parties

Where several opposite parties are involved, their locations and the applicable requirement for permission of the commission must be independently examined.

Ahmedabad Consumer Proceedings: Consumer complaints may be instituted before the competent Ahmedabad Consumer Commission where the applicable territorial, pecuniary and subject-matter requirements are satisfied.
Section 69

Limitation for Consumer Complaints

2 Years

Ordinary Limitation Period

A consumer complaint must ordinarily be instituted within two years from accrual of the cause of action.

Sufficient Cause

Delay Condonation

A delayed complaint may be admitted upon sufficient cause being established and reasons for condonation being recorded by the competent commission.

Cause of Action

Independent Assessment

The legally relevant accrual date depends upon the nature of the obligation, alleged deficiency and relevant factual circumstances.

Repeated reminders, internal grievances, legal notices and subsequent correspondence do not automatically extend or revive the statutory limitation period. A continuing cause of action must be established independently on the facts and applicable judicial principles.
Section 39

Reliefs Available Before Consumer Commissions

Monetary Remedies

  • Refund of price or charges paid.
  • Interest, where legally appropriate.
  • Compensation for established loss or injury.
  • Product liability compensation.
  • Punitive damages in appropriate circumstances.
  • Reasonable litigation costs.

Corrective & Non-Monetary Remedies

  • Removal of defects in goods.
  • Replacement with goods free from defects.
  • Removal of deficiency in services.
  • Discontinuation of unfair trade practices.
  • Corrective advertisements, where applicable.
  • Directions concerning hazardous goods or services.
Compensation is determined judicially. The amount claimed by a complainant is not automatically payable merely because a consumer complaint has been filed. Relief must be supported by applicable law, factual pleadings and relevant evidence.

Further Proceedings

Appeals, Reviews & Enforcement

Appeal against District Commission Order

Section 41 provides an appeal before the State Commission, ordinarily within forty-five days from the date of the order.

A person required to pay an amount under the order must comply with the applicable fifty per cent statutory deposit requirement.

Appeal against State Commission Order

Section 51 provides for appeals before the National Commission against specified State Commission orders.

Appeals from qualifying original jurisdiction orders ordinarily carry a thirty-day limitation period and applicable statutory deposit requirements. Appeals from appellate orders involve additional statutory conditions.

Appeal before the Supreme Court

Section 67 provides for appeals against specified original jurisdiction orders of the National Commission, ordinarily within thirty days.

Other challenges require examination of the applicable constitutional and statutory remedies.

Review & Revision

Sections 40, 50 and 60 provide review powers concerning errors apparent on the face of the record.

Revisional jurisdiction is distinct from appellate jurisdiction and is subject to the statutory grounds prescribed under the Act.

Execution under Section 71

Consumer Commission orders may be enforced in the manner prescribed for execution of civil court decrees, with applicable provisions of Order XXI of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908.

Non-Compliance under Section 72

Failure to comply with an enforceable Consumer Commission order may attract statutory penal consequences under Section 72.

Such proceedings remain subject to the statutory requirements and judicial determination.

Legal Awareness

Frequently Asked Questions

Who can file a consumer complaint?

Eligible consumers, recognised consumer associations, qualifying representative consumers, specified government authorities and other persons recognised by the Act may institute complaints in accordance with statutory requirements.

Can a complaint be filed against a bank?

Yes, where the complainant qualifies as a consumer and the dispute concerns a legally recognised banking service deficiency or other maintainable consumer ground.

Can a complaint be filed against an insurance company?

Eligible disputes concerning claim repudiation, delays, policy obligations and insurance service deficiencies may be brought before the competent Consumer Commission.

Can a housing finance company be challenged before a Consumer Commission?

Yes, where the consumer relationship and necessary statutory grounds are established. The nature of the lending dispute and availability of specialised statutory remedies must also be considered.

Is every increase in loan interest or tenure unlawful?

No. The loan agreement, applicable regulatory directions, disclosure obligations and communication of revised repayment terms must be examined before determining whether deficiency in service has occurred.

Can wrongful insurance claim rejection attract compensation?

Where wrongful repudiation or deficiency is established, the competent commission may award legally permissible relief, including claim amounts, interest, compensation and costs where justified.

Is a legal notice compulsory before filing a consumer complaint?

There is no general mandatory pre-litigation legal notice requirement for all consumer complaints under the Act. However, prior communication may be relevant to the facts, contractual obligations and evidence.

What is the limitation period?

Section 69 ordinarily prescribes two years from accrual of the cause of action. Delay may be condoned upon sufficient cause being established.

Does sending a legal notice restart limitation?

Not automatically. Limitation depends upon the legally relevant cause of action and applicable statutory and judicial principles.

Does the compensation claimed determine pecuniary jurisdiction?

No. Ordinary original pecuniary jurisdiction is determined by the legally relevant consideration paid for goods or services under the applicable jurisdiction rules.

What is the District Commission monetary limit?

Under the 2021 Jurisdiction Rules, the ordinary original complaint threshold is consideration paid not exceeding fifty lakh rupees, subject to other statutory provisions.

Can a consumer complaint be filed where the complainant resides?

Yes, where the applicable territorial jurisdiction provisions permit it. Other requirements concerning pecuniary jurisdiction and maintainability must also be fulfilled.

Can a consumer complaint be filed electronically?

Yes. Section 35 permits electronic filing in the prescribed manner. Availability and requirements of the applicable official filing system should be verified.

Is representation by an advocate compulsory?

No. Eligible consumers may represent themselves before Consumer Commissions. Legal representation may assist with complex pleadings, evidence, jurisdictional issues and legal arguments.

Can compensation for mental harassment be claimed?

Compensation may be claimed for legally recognised loss or injury, including mental agony or harassment where supported by the facts and applicable judicial principles.

Can a defective vehicle be replaced?

Replacement is a potential statutory remedy in appropriate circumstances. The defect, supporting evidence, contractual warranty and nature of the established liability must be examined.

Can a homebuyer approach both RERA and a Consumer Commission?

Consumer remedies may coexist with remedies under real estate legislation, subject to statutory eligibility, applicable judicial principles and avoidance of inconsistent or duplicate recovery.

Can a commercial transaction qualify as a consumer dispute?

Transactions for commercial purposes are generally excluded, subject to statutory exceptions and judicial interpretation. The dominant purpose and circumstances of the transaction require independent examination.

Can consumer disputes be settled through mediation?

Yes. Eligible disputes may be referred to mediation in accordance with the Act, applicable rules, consent requirements and exclusions.

What happens if the opposite party fails to comply with the final order?

Enforcement proceedings may be initiated under Section 71. Eligible non-compliance may also attract proceedings under Section 72, subject to statutory requirements.

Is compensation guaranteed in every consumer complaint?

No. Relief depends upon statutory maintainability, pleadings, evidence, established deficiency or other actionable conduct and judicial determination.

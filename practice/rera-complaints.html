Real Estate Regulation and Homebuyer Rights

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 establishes a statutory framework for regulation of the real estate sector, protection of allottee interests, transparency in project development and adjudication of specified real estate disputes.

The Gujarat Real Estate Regulatory Authority exercises statutory functions in relation to real estate projects and transactions falling within its jurisdiction.

Disputes may arise from delayed possession, non-completion of projects, deviations from sanctioned plans, incorrect representations, additional demands, construction defects and failure to comply with contractual or statutory obligations.

MNM Legal Associates, based in Chandkheda, Ahmedabad, deals with real estate disputes, property-related proceedings, RERA complaints, contractual obligations and associated legal documentation.

Each matter requires examination of the project registration status, agreement for sale, payment records, possession commitments, statutory disclosures and applicable regulatory requirements.

Statutory Safeguards

Important Protections under RERA

10%

Advance Payment Restriction

Under Section 13, a promoter cannot accept more than ten per cent of the cost as advance or application fee without first entering into a written agreement for sale and registering it as required by law.

70%

Separate Project Account

Section 4(2)(l)(D) requires seventy per cent of amounts realised from allottees to be deposited in a separate account for specified project costs, subject to statutory withdrawal and certification requirements.

2/3

Consent for Specified Alterations

Section 14(2)(ii) requires prior written consent of at least two-thirds of the relevant allottees, excluding the promoter, for specified alterations or additions.

5 Years

Defect Liability Period

Section 14(3) addresses specified defects brought to the promoter's notice within five years from handing over possession.

30 Days

Defect Rectification

Qualifying defects under Section 14(3) must be rectified without further charge within thirty days, failing which compensation may be available.

60 Days

Appellate Tribunal Appeal

Section 44 generally prescribes sixty days from receipt of a copy of the relevant order for filing an appeal, subject to permissible condonation.

Section 18

Refund, Interest and Compensation

The available relief depends upon the nature of the promoter's default, the allottee's election and the applicable statutory conditions.

Option 1 — Withdrawal from the Project

Where the statutory conditions under Section 18(1) are satisfied, an allottee who wishes to withdraw from the project may seek return of the amounts received by the promoter with prescribed interest and compensation in accordance with law.

  • Refund of qualifying amounts paid.
  • Applicable prescribed interest.
  • Compensation through the appropriate forum.

Option 2 — Continue with the Project

An allottee who does not intend to withdraw may claim interest for every month of qualifying delay until handing over possession, at the prescribed rate.

  • Continuation of the allotment.
  • Interest for the legally relevant delay.
  • Delivery of possession in accordance with law.

Defective Title

Section 18(2) provides for compensation for loss caused by defective title of the land on which the project is being developed or has been developed.

The provision expressly addresses limitation in relation to such claims.

Other Promoter Defaults

Section 18(3) provides for compensation where a promoter fails to discharge other obligations imposed by the Act, rules, regulations or agreement for sale, subject to applicable requirements.

Jurisdictional distinction: Refund and statutory interest claims and claims for adjudication of compensation must be presented before the appropriate statutory forum according to the nature of the relief sought.

Rights and Responsibilities

Rights of Allottees and Obligations of Promoters

Rights of Allottees

Section 19 provides specified rights to allottees, including:

  • Information concerning sanctioned plans and specifications.
  • Information regarding stage-wise project completion.
  • Possession in accordance with applicable obligations.
  • Refund, interest and compensation where legally available.
  • Access to prescribed documents and plans after possession.

Obligations of Allottees

  • Payment according to the agreement for sale.
  • Payment of applicable interest for qualifying defaults.
  • Participation in formation of the association or society.
  • Taking possession within the applicable statutory period.
  • Participation in registration of the conveyance deed.

Obligations of Promoters

  • Compliance with registered project disclosures.
  • Adherence to sanctioned plans and specifications.
  • Obtaining applicable completion or occupancy certificates.
  • Performance of contractual and statutory obligations.
  • Execution of conveyance and handover of documents.
  • Rectification of qualifying defects.

Obligations of Real Estate Agents

  • Registration where required under the Act.
  • Avoidance of transactions prohibited by Section 10.
  • Maintenance of prescribed records.
  • Avoidance of unfair trade practices.
  • Facilitation of relevant information and documentation.
Jurisdiction

RERA Authority, Adjudicating Officer and Appellate Tribunal

Forum Principal Function Relevant Provisions
Real Estate Regulatory Authority Regulatory functions, statutory complaints, refund and interest relief within its jurisdiction. Sections 31, 34, 35, 36, 37 and 38
Adjudicating Officer Adjudication of compensation under Sections 12, 14, 18 and 19. Sections 71 and 72
Real Estate Appellate Tribunal Appeals against qualifying orders, decisions or directions of the Authority or Adjudicating Officer. Sections 43 and 44
High Court Appeals against qualifying Appellate Tribunal decisions on grounds specified under Section 100 CPC. Section 58
The Supreme Court in Newtech Promoters and Developers Pvt. Ltd. v. State of Uttar Pradesh distinguished the Authority's jurisdiction concerning refund and interest from the Adjudicating Officer's jurisdiction to adjudge compensation.
Appellate Proceedings

Appeal Periods and Promoter Pre-Deposit

60 Days

Section 44 Appeal

Appeal before the Appellate Tribunal from receipt of a copy of the relevant order, subject to condonation where sufficient cause is established.

30%+

Penalty Pre-Deposit

Where applicable, a promoter's appeal requires deposit of at least thirty per cent of the penalty, or a higher percentage determined by the Tribunal.

Full Amount

Allottee Award Pre-Deposit

Section 43(5) requires deposit of the total amount payable to the allottee, including interest and compensation, if any, where applicable, or both categories of deposit as the case may be.

60 Days

High Court Appeal

Section 58 provides for an appeal within sixty days from communication of the Appellate Tribunal's decision, subject to statutory conditions and permissible condonation.

(2020) 16 SCC 512

Consumer compensation principles concerning delayed possession and the effect of contractual compensation clauses.

Refund and interest remedies in qualifying consumer disputes arising from real estate transactions.

Legal Awareness

Frequently Asked Questions

What is RERA?

RERA refers to the statutory framework established under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, for regulation of real estate projects, protection of allottee interests and adjudication of specified disputes.

Who can file a RERA complaint?

Section 31 permits an aggrieved person to file a complaint concerning violations of the Act, rules or regulations before the appropriate Authority or Adjudicating Officer, as applicable.

Can I file a complaint against a builder for delayed possession?

Qualifying delay in possession may give rise to remedies under Section 18, including refund or delay interest, depending upon the allottee's election and applicable statutory conditions.

Can I recover the entire amount paid to the builder?

Where Section 18(1) applies and the allottee elects to withdraw, return of qualifying amounts received by the promoter with prescribed interest may be available.

Can I claim interest without cancelling my flat booking?

Yes. The proviso to Section 18(1) provides for interest for every month of qualifying delay where the allottee continues with the project.

What is the applicable RERA interest rate in Gujarat?

The applicable rate must be determined under the relevant Gujarat RERA rules, amendments and orders. It should not be assumed to be a permanently fixed percentage.

Can a builder collect more than ten per cent before executing an agreement?

Section 13 restricts acceptance of more than ten per cent of the cost as advance or application fee without first entering into a written agreement for sale and registering it as required.

Can a builder change the sanctioned building plan?

Section 14 restricts alterations to sanctioned plans and specifications. Applicable consent requirements and statutory exceptions depend upon the nature of the proposed alteration.

Can I complain about construction defects after possession?

Section 14(3) provides remedies for qualifying defects brought to the promoter's notice within five years from handing over possession.

How much time does a builder have to rectify qualifying defects?

Section 14(3) prescribes rectification without further charge within thirty days, failing which appropriate compensation may be available.

Can I claim compensation for financial loss?

Compensation may be available under applicable provisions of the Act. The nature of the loss, evidence, statutory entitlement and jurisdiction of the Adjudicating Officer must be examined.

Can I file a complaint against an unregistered project?

Failure to register a project that is legally required to be registered may constitute statutory non-compliance. Applicable exemptions and project circumstances must first be examined.

Does RERA apply to every real estate project?

No. Section 3(2) contains specified exemptions, including qualifying small projects, projects with completion certificates obtained before commencement and specified renovation or redevelopment activities. Applicable state requirements must also be examined.

Can a homebuyer approach the Consumer Commission instead of RERA?

Consumer remedies may remain available where the complainant satisfies the applicable consumer law requirements. Forum selection depends upon the facts and relief sought.

Can a builder appeal against a RERA order?

A promoter may file an appeal against a qualifying order, subject to limitation, maintainability and statutory pre-deposit requirements under Section 43(5).

What happens if a builder does not comply with a RERA order?

Enforcement and recovery proceedings may be available under Section 40 and other applicable provisions. The appropriate mechanism depends upon the nature of the order.

Does an extension of RERA registration automatically defeat a delay claim?

Not necessarily. The agreement for sale, statutory extension, relevant orders and circumstances of delay must be independently examined.

Can a housing society file a RERA complaint?

An eligible association of allottees or other aggrieved person may invoke Section 31 where the complaint falls within the statutory framework.

Is there a universal limitation period for filing a RERA complaint?

Section 31 does not itself prescribe a universal numerical limitation period. Applicable limitation principles, the nature of the claim, delay and relevant judicial decisions require independent assessment.

Can a RERA complaint be filed in Gujarat for a project in Ahmedabad?

Projects in Ahmedabad falling within the Gujarat RERA framework may be subject to proceedings before the competent statutory forum, provided applicable jurisdictional requirements are satisfied.

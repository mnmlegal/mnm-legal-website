Copyright Protection and Enforcement in India

Copyright is a statutory right protecting qualifying original literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works, cinematograph films and sound recordings.

The Copyright Act, 1957 confers different exclusive rights depending upon the nature of the protected work, including rights relating to reproduction, publication, adaptation, communication to the public and other specified uses.

Copyright protection generally arises without compulsory registration, subject to satisfaction of the applicable statutory requirements.

Ownership, authorship, assignment, licensing and statutory exceptions must be independently examined before determining whether a particular use is authorised or constitutes infringement.

MNM Legal Associates, based in Chandkheda, Ahmedabad, deals with copyright-related legal matters, intellectual property documentation, licensing disputes and associated civil and criminal proceedings.

Music and Entertainment Law

Sound Recording and Public Performance Rights

Commercial use of music may involve multiple independent rights in the recording and its underlying creative works.

Sound Recording Copyright

Section 14(e) specifies exclusive rights in sound recordings.

  • Making another sound recording embodying it, including specified electronic storage.
  • Sale or commercial rental rights as provided by the Act.
  • Communication of the sound recording to the public under Section 14(e)(iii).

Underlying Musical and Literary Works

Copyright in a sound recording is distinct from copyright in qualifying underlying musical and literary works.

  • Musical compositions.
  • Lyrics and literary works.
  • Applicable author and royalty rights.
  • Independent ownership and licensing arrangements.

Communication to the Public

Section 2(ff) defines communication to the public in relation to making a work or performance available for being seen, heard or otherwise enjoyed by the public, subject to the statutory definition.

Actual enjoyment by individual members of the public is not necessarily required for the statutory definition to apply.

Licensing Authority

The authority of a person or entity to grant a licence must be examined with reference to ownership, assignment, authorisation, repertoire and applicable statutory provisions.

Sections 30 and 33 must be considered together with relevant judicial decisions and the particular licensing arrangement.

Important distinction: A licence concerning one category of copyright does not automatically authorise every other category of protected rights. The scope of the licence, ownership of the relevant repertoire, applicable statutory exceptions and nature of the actual use must be independently examined.

Limitations and Exceptions

Acts Not Constituting Copyright Infringement

Section 52 identifies specified acts that do not constitute copyright infringement when their statutory requirements are satisfied.

Fair Dealing

Section 52(1)(a) provides specified fair dealing exceptions, other than for computer programs, concerning private or personal use including research, criticism, review and reporting of current events or affairs.

Educational Uses

Certain reproduction and performance activities connected with instruction, examinations and educational institutions may qualify under specific provisions of Section 52.

Judicial Proceedings

Specified reproduction of works for judicial proceedings or reporting of judicial proceedings may fall within statutory exceptions.

Religious and Official Ceremonies

Section 52(1)(za) provides an exception for specified performances or communication to the public in the course of bona fide religious ceremonies or qualifying official ceremonies.

The statutory explanation includes marriage processions and other social festivities associated with a marriage within religious ceremonies.

Computer Program Exceptions

Specified acts concerning lawful possession, use, backup, interoperability and study of computer programs may qualify under applicable provisions of Section 52.

Accessible Formats

The Act provides specified exceptions concerning adaptation, reproduction and communication of works in accessible formats for persons with disabilities, subject to statutory conditions.

Copyright exceptions are fact-specific. Non-commercial use, attribution, educational purpose or the absence of an admission fee does not automatically establish that a particular activity falls within Section 52. Equally, the involvement of a commercial venue does not by itself eliminate an otherwise applicable statutory exception.

Criminal Statutory Framework

Criminal Liability under the Copyright Act

Section 63 — Knowing Infringement

Section 63 generally prescribes imprisonment of not less than six months, which may extend to three years, and a fine of not less than fifty thousand rupees, which may extend to two lakh rupees.

The statutory proviso permits a lesser sentence for adequate and special reasons in specified circumstances where infringement was not made for gain in the course of trade or business.

Section 63A — Enhanced Punishment

Qualifying second and subsequent convictions under Section 63 may attract imprisonment of not less than one year and up to three years, together with a fine of not less than one lakh rupees and up to two lakh rupees.

The provision is subject to its statutory conditions, proviso and exceptions.

Cognizable and Non-Bailable Offence

In Knit Pro International v. State (NCT of Delhi), the Supreme Court held that an offence under Section 63 is cognizable and non-bailable under the applicable criminal procedural classification.

Current procedural requirements must also be examined under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 and applicable judicial decisions.

Section 64 — Police Powers

A police officer not below the rank of sub-inspector may exercise the specified seizure power without warrant where the statutory satisfaction requirements are met.

Seized infringing copies and plates must be produced before a Magistrate as soon as practicable in accordance with the provision.

Criminal liability under Section 63 requires examination of the statutory ingredients, including knowing infringement or abetment. An allegation of unauthorised use does not by itself establish criminal guilt. Cognizable and non-bailable classification does not mean that arrest is mandatory or that bail is legally unavailable.

Legal Awareness

Frequently Asked Questions

What is copyright?

Copyright is a statutory right protecting qualifying creative works and conferring specified exclusive rights upon their owners.

Is copyright registration compulsory in India?

No. Copyright protection generally arises without compulsory registration, subject to statutory requirements. Registration provides evidentiary advantages under the Act.

Can an idea be protected by copyright?

Copyright generally protects the original expression of an idea, rather than the abstract idea itself.

Can software be protected by copyright?

Yes. Computer programs fall within the statutory definition of literary works and are subject to applicable copyright protection.

Who is the first owner of copyright?

The author is generally the first owner, subject to the exceptions, contractual arrangements and requirements of Section 17.

Can copyright ownership be transferred?

Yes. Copyright may be assigned subject to Sections 18 and 19, including applicable statutory restrictions and royalty protections.

What is the difference between assignment and licensing?

Assignment transfers specified copyright ownership interests. A licence authorises specified use without necessarily transferring ownership of copyright.

Is a sound recording different from a musical work?

Yes. A sound recording and its underlying musical and literary works are distinct categories of protected works with potentially separate rights and ownership.

What is communication to the public?

Section 2(ff) defines communication to the public by reference to making a work or performance available for public enjoyment, subject to the complete statutory definition.

Is a music licence required for background music in a business?

Commercial background music may involve exclusive copyright rights requiring authorisation. The actual requirement depends on the work, ownership, existing licences, usage and applicable statutory exceptions.

Does a personal music streaming subscription authorise commercial use?

A personal subscription does not automatically authorise public or commercial exploitation. The subscription terms and relevant copyright permissions must be examined.

Does one music licence cover all copyright rights?

Not necessarily. A licence covers the rights and repertoire that the licensor is authorised to grant within the licence terms.

Is copyright licensing required for marriage ceremonies?

Section 52(1)(za) provides a statutory exception for specified uses in bona fide religious ceremonies, including marriage processions and associated social festivities as defined by the Act. The particular facts must satisfy the statutory requirements.

Can a venue owner and event organiser both be liable?

Potential liability depends upon the statutory ingredients, the respective involvement of each person, permissions, knowledge where relevant and applicable exceptions or defences. Liability is not automatic.

Is a legal notice compulsory before copyright enforcement?

The Copyright Act does not prescribe a universal prior-notice requirement for every infringement proceeding. Applicable procedural requirements, contractual obligations and the circumstances of the proposed proceeding must nevertheless be examined.

Can copyright infringement result in criminal proceedings?

Yes. Section 63 and other applicable provisions prescribe criminal consequences where the necessary statutory ingredients are established.

Is an offence under Section 63 non-bailable?

The Supreme Court in Knit Pro International classified the offence as cognizable and non-bailable under the applicable procedural law. Non-bailable classification does not prohibit the grant of bail by a competent authority.

Can the police seize infringing copies without a warrant?

Section 64 provides a specified seizure power to police officers not below the rank of sub-inspector, subject to statutory conditions and procedural safeguards.

Can damages be claimed for copyright infringement?

Section 55 provides civil remedies, including damages and accounts, subject to applicable statutory conditions and judicial assessment.

Does giving credit to the original author prevent infringement?

Attribution alone does not automatically authorise use. Permission, licence terms and applicable statutory exceptions must be considered.

Can an author retain moral rights after assignment?

Section 57 recognises specified special rights of authors, independently of copyright and even after assignment, subject to statutory requirements.

Does copyright protection last forever?

No. Copyright duration varies according to the category of work, relevant dates and applicable statutory provisions.

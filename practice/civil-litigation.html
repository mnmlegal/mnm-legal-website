Our Practice

Civil Rights and Judicial Remedies

Civil litigation concerns the determination, protection and enforcement of legal rights, obligations and interests through proceedings before competent courts and judicial forums.

MNM Legal Associates, based in Chandkheda, Ahmedabad, deals with civil disputes involving contractual obligations, property interests, recovery of money, declarations, injunctions, specific performance and execution proceedings.

Effective assessment of a civil dispute requires examination of the cause of action, documentary evidence, territorial and pecuniary jurisdiction, limitation, valuation, applicable court fees and the nature of relief sought.

Civil remedies may include interim protection, final adjudication, enforcement of decrees and legally recognised methods of alternative dispute resolution.

Principal Civil Laws

Code of Civil Procedure, 1908

Institution of suits, pleadings, interim applications, trial, execution and appeals.

Specific Relief Act, 1963

Specific performance, declaration, injunctions and other specific remedies.

Indian Contract Act, 1872

Contractual rights, obligations, breach and compensation.

Limitation Act, 1963

Prescribed limitation periods for suits, appeals and applications.

Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023

Relevancy, admissibility and proof of documentary, oral and electronic evidence.

Scope of Practice

Civil Law Matters

Principal categories of civil proceedings and corresponding statutory remedies. Expand each category to explore its scope.

Civil Suits

Sections 9 & 26 and Orders IV & VII CPC

Institution and defence of civil suits concerning enforceable legal rights, obligations and civil disputes.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Drafting and institution of plaints.
  • Written statements and counterclaims.
  • Jurisdictional objections.
  • Interim applications.
  • Trial and final arguments.

Temporary Injunction

Order XXXIX Rules 1 & 2 CPC

Interim judicial protection intended to preserve rights or property during the pendency of civil proceedings.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Temporary injunction applications.
  • Ad-interim injunction applications.
  • Status quo orders.
  • Protection against dispossession.
  • Restraint against alienation.
  • Proceedings under Order XXXIX Rule 2A.

Permanent Injunction

Sections 37 & 38, Specific Relief Act

Final judicial relief restraining interference with legally recognised rights or breach of enforceable obligations.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Perpetual injunction suits.
  • Protection of lawful possession.
  • Prevention of interference with civil rights.
  • Restraint against breach of obligations.
  • Enforcement of injunction decrees.

Mandatory Injunction

Section 39, Specific Relief Act

Judicial directions requiring performance of specified acts where legally necessary to prevent breach of an obligation.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Removal of unlawful obstruction.
  • Restoration of affected rights.
  • Performance of specified legal obligations.
  • Appropriate consequential relief.

Declaratory Suits

Section 34, Specific Relief Act

Judicial determination of legal character or rights relating to property, together with consequential relief where required.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Declaration of legal status.
  • Declaration of ownership and title.
  • Declaration concerning disputed rights.
  • Declaration with consequential injunction.
  • Other appropriate consequential remedies.

Specific Performance

Sections 10, 14 & 16, Specific Relief Act

Proceedings seeking enforcement of contractual obligations through specific performance, subject to statutory requirements.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Agreement-to-sell disputes.
  • Enforcement of eligible contracts.
  • Readiness and willingness requirements.
  • Consequential possession or other relief.
  • Defence against specific performance claims.

Money Recovery Suits

CPC & Indian Contract Act, 1872

Civil proceedings for recovery of legally enforceable debts, outstanding payments and contractual monetary claims.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Recovery of personal and business loans.
  • Outstanding invoices and payments.
  • Recovery of contractual dues.
  • Interest claims, where legally permissible.
  • Enforcement of money decrees.

Summary Suits

Order XXXVII CPC

Special summary procedure applicable to specified classes of monetary claims and written contractual obligations.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Eligible written contract claims.
  • Bills of exchange and promissory notes.
  • Specified liquidated monetary demands.
  • Summons for judgment.
  • Applications for leave to defend.

Contractual Disputes

Indian Contract Act, 1872

Disputes arising from interpretation, performance, breach and termination of contractual arrangements.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Breach of contract.
  • Non-performance of agreements.
  • Compensation under Sections 73 and 74.
  • Refund and restitution claims.
  • Commercial and service agreement disputes.

Cancellation of Instruments

Section 31, Specific Relief Act

Proceedings seeking cancellation of void or voidable written instruments where statutory conditions are satisfied.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Disputed sale deeds.
  • Fraudulently obtained instruments.
  • Void or voidable agreements.
  • Cancellation of disputed documents.
  • Consequential declaratory relief.

Recovery of Possession

Sections 5 & 6, Specific Relief Act

Civil proceedings concerning recovery of immovable property and remedies against unlawful dispossession.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Title-based possession suits.
  • Remedies against unlawful dispossession.
  • Recovery of immovable property.
  • Mesne profits and related claims.
  • Consequential injunction proceedings.

Partition & Co-ownership Disputes

Order XX Rule 18 CPC & Applicable Laws

Proceedings concerning determination of shares, division of jointly held property and separate possession.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Partition suits.
  • Determination of ownership shares.
  • Preliminary and final decrees.
  • Separate possession.
  • Protection of disputed property interests.

Execution Proceedings

Sections 36–74 & Order XXI CPC

Judicial enforcement of decrees and executable orders through legally prescribed execution procedures.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Execution of money decrees.
  • Attachment and sale of property.
  • Delivery of possession.
  • Execution of injunction decrees.
  • Execution objections.
  • Transfer of decrees for execution.

Civil Appeals

Sections 96, 100 & 104 CPC

Appellate proceedings challenging civil decrees and appealable orders, subject to statutory maintainability and limitation.

Scope of Proceedings
  • First appeals under Section 96 CPC.
  • Second appeals on substantial questions of law.
  • Appeals against specified orders.
  • Applications for stay of execution.
  • Appellate pleadings and arguments.

Review & Revision

Sections 114 & 115 and Order XLVII CPC

Limited statutory remedies concerning review of judgments and revisional jurisdiction in legally maintainable cases.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Review applications.
  • Discovery of qualifying new evidence.
  • Error apparent on the face of the record.
  • Civil revision applications.
  • Jurisdictional irregularities.

Commercial Disputes

Commercial Courts Act, 2015

Proceedings concerning qualifying commercial disputes of specified value before competent commercial courts.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Commercial contract disputes.
  • Business transaction disputes.
  • Commercial recovery proceedings.
  • Pre-institution mediation, where mandatory.
  • Commercial summary judgment applications.

Caveat Proceedings

Section 148A CPC

Lodging a caveat where a person claims a right to appear in relation to an anticipated or pending application.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Drafting and filing of caveats.
  • Service upon anticipated applicants.
  • Protection of opportunity to be heard.
  • Representation in related applications.
  • Monitoring statutory caveat validity.

Mediation & Civil Settlement

Section 89 & Order XXIII Rule 3 CPC

Legally recognised methods of resolving civil disputes through negotiation, mediation and lawful compromise.

Scope of Proceedings
  • Settlement negotiations.
  • Court-referred mediation.
  • Drafting settlement terms.
  • Lawful compromise applications.
  • Recording and enforcement of compromise decrees.
Interlocutory Proceedings

Interim Protection and Procedural Remedies

Civil courts may grant appropriate interim relief where the applicable legal requirements and circumstances justify judicial intervention.

Temporary Injunction

Order XXXIX CPC provides for temporary injunctions in appropriate circumstances, ordinarily involving consideration of a prima facie case, balance of convenience and irreparable injury.

Attachment Before Judgment

Order XXXVIII Rule 5 CPC provides a conditional remedy against attempts to obstruct or delay execution of a potential decree by disposing of or removing property.

Appointment of Receiver

Order XL CPC permits appointment of a receiver where the court considers such appointment just and convenient.

Local Investigation

Order XXVI Rule 9 CPC provides for appointment of a commission for local investigation in appropriate matters.

Amendment of Pleadings

Order VI Rule 17 CPC permits amendment of pleadings subject to applicable requirements, including the due diligence requirement after commencement of trial.

Rejection of Plaint

Order VII Rule 11 CPC provides for rejection of a plaint on specified statutory grounds, including where the plaint discloses no cause of action or appears to be barred by law.

Judicial Process

Stages of a Civil Suit

The following represents the general procedural framework of an ordinary civil suit. The actual procedure may vary according to the nature of proceedings and applicable statutory requirements.

01

Pre-litigation Assessment

Examination of facts, documents, cause of action, limitation, jurisdiction and available remedies.

02

Notice & Pre-institution Requirements

Issuance of legal notice or compliance with mandatory pre-institution requirements, wherever applicable.

03

Institution of Suit

Filing of plaint, supporting documents, appropriate valuation, court fees and necessary interim applications.

04

Summons & Written Statement

Service of summons and filing of defence, objections, set-off or counterclaim, as legally applicable.

05

Interim Applications

Consideration of interim relief and procedural applications during the pendency of the suit.

06

Framing of Issues

Identification of disputed material propositions of fact and law requiring judicial determination.

07

Evidence & Cross-examination

Production and proof of documentary, oral and electronic evidence, including examination of witnesses.

08

Arguments, Judgment & Decree

Final submissions followed by judicial determination of the disputed issues and reliefs claimed.

09

Execution & Further Remedies

Enforcement of the decree or pursuit of available appellate, review or revisional remedies.

Legal Awareness

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a civil suit?

A civil suit is a judicial proceeding instituted for determination or enforcement of civil rights, obligations or interests. Section 9 CPC recognises the jurisdiction of civil courts over suits of a civil nature unless their cognizance is expressly or impliedly barred.

Is a legal notice mandatory before filing a civil suit?

A legal notice is not mandatory before every civil suit. However, certain statutes prescribe mandatory notice or pre-institution requirements. For example, Section 80 CPC generally requires prior notice in specified suits against the Government or public officers, subject to statutory exceptions.

What is the limitation period for filing a civil suit?

The limitation period depends upon the nature of the claim and the applicable article of the Limitation Act, 1963. Many contractual and monetary claims carry a three-year limitation period, but the starting point and applicable period vary. There is no uniform limitation period for all civil suits.

What is the difference between temporary and permanent injunction?

A temporary injunction operates for a specified period or until further judicial orders during proceedings. A permanent or perpetual injunction is granted by decree upon adjudication of the merits and permanently restrains the defendant from specified conduct.

Can a civil court grant an injunction without hearing the opposite party?

In appropriate circumstances, an ex parte temporary injunction may be granted where delay would defeat the object of the injunction. Order XXXIX Rule 3 CPC prescribes requirements including recording reasons and compliance with service obligations.

What is a summary suit under Order XXXVII CPC?

A summary suit is a special procedure available for specified classes of claims, including certain written contractual monetary demands. Unlike an ordinary suit, the defendant must comply with the prescribed appearance and leave-to-defend procedure.

What is the purpose of filing a caveat?

A caveat under Section 148A CPC is intended to secure an opportunity of being heard in relation to an anticipated or pending application. It generally remains in force for ninety days from the date of lodging, unless the relevant application is made before expiry of that period.

How are court fees calculated in a civil suit?

Court fees depend upon the nature of relief, valuation of the suit and applicable court fee legislation. In Gujarat, the Gujarat Court-fees Act, 2004, together with applicable amendments and rules, must be considered.

Can a civil dispute be settled after filing a suit?

Yes. A civil dispute may be resolved through lawful settlement or mediation. Order XXIII Rule 3 CPC provides for recording a lawful compromise satisfying the statutory requirements and passing a decree accordingly.

Does filing a civil appeal automatically stay execution?

No. Under Order XLI Rule 5 CPC, filing an appeal does not by itself operate as a stay of execution. A separate stay order must ordinarily be obtained from the competent court.

Is obtaining a civil decree sufficient to recover money?

A decree establishes the adjudicated entitlement. Where the judgment debtor does not voluntarily comply, execution proceedings may be required under Order XXI CPC and other applicable provisions.

Which civil court has jurisdiction in Ahmedabad?

Jurisdiction depends upon the subject matter, territorial limits, valuation and applicable enactments. Depending upon the dispute, proceedings may fall before a competent civil court, City Civil Court, Commercial Court, District Court, designated tribunal or the High Court of Gujarat.

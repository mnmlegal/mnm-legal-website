Cheque Dishonour Proceedings under Section 138

The Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 regulates negotiable instruments, including cheques, promissory notes and bills of exchange. Chapter XVII of the Act provides a statutory framework concerning certain instances of cheque dishonour.

Section 138 creates criminal liability where a cheque issued towards an enforceable debt or liability is dishonoured and the prescribed statutory conditions are fulfilled.

MNM Legal Associates, based in Chandkheda, Ahmedabad, deals with cheque dishonour matters, statutory demand notices, complaints before competent courts, defence representation, compensation applications and related proceedings.

Cheque dishonour matters require careful examination of the underlying transaction, cheque validity, bank return memorandum, service of notice, limitation, territorial jurisdiction and available documentary evidence.

The mere return of a cheque does not automatically establish criminal liability. Each statutory requirement must be considered in light of the facts and applicable law.

Statutory Requirements

Section 138 Statutory Timeline

Cheque dishonour proceedings involve distinct statutory periods. Each period must be calculated from the legally relevant triggering event.

3 Months

Cheque Presentation

Present the cheque within its applicable validity period. Ordinary cheque validity under prevailing RBI directions is three months.

30 Days

Statutory Demand Notice

Issue the written statutory demand within thirty days of receiving dishonour information from the bank.

15 Days

Payment Opportunity

The drawer is allowed fifteen days following receipt or legally recognised service of the notice to make payment.

1 Month

Complaint Filing

Institute the complaint within one calendar month from accrual of the statutory cause of action.

Important: The complaint limitation is one calendar month, not necessarily thirty days. The statutory cause of action ordinarily arises on the day following expiry of the fifteen-day payment period. Delay in filing the complaint may be condoned upon sufficient cause under Section 142(1)(b). This provision does not generally authorise condonation of delay in issuing the statutory notice.

Legal Requirements

Essential Ingredients of Section 138

Drawing of Cheque

The cheque must have been drawn on an account maintained by the drawer, subject to statutory provisions concerning corporate drawers.

Legally Enforceable Liability

The instrument must relate to an enforceable debt or other liability, either wholly or in part. Statutory presumptions and admissible evidence are relevant to this determination.

Presentation within Validity

The cheque must have been presented within the period prescribed by Section 138(a), read with the applicable cheque validity requirements.

Dishonour of Cheque

The cheque must have been returned unpaid on a ground attracting Section 138, as interpreted under applicable judicial principles.

Written Statutory Demand

The payee or holder in due course must issue a written demand for payment of the cheque amount within the prescribed statutory period.

Failure to Make Payment

The drawer must fail to make the required payment within fifteen days following receipt or legally recognised service of notice.

Punishment under Section 138: Upon conviction, the court may impose imprisonment extending up to two years, a fine extending up to twice the cheque amount, or both, subject to applicable law. The maximum statutory punishment does not apply automatically to every case.
Section 142(2)

Territorial Jurisdiction in Cheque Bounce Cases

Collection through Bank Account

Where the cheque is delivered for collection through an account, jurisdiction ordinarily lies where the bank branch maintaining the payee's or holder in due course's account is situated.

Depositing the cheque at another branch does not necessarily change the statutory account-maintaining branch for jurisdictional purposes.

Presentation Otherwise through an Account

Where the cheque is presented otherwise than through an account, jurisdiction ordinarily lies where the drawee bank branch maintaining the drawer's account is situated.

Related complaints involving the same drawer may also require examination of Section 142A and applicable transfer provisions.

The location of the underlying transaction, residence of the parties or place where the demand notice was drafted does not independently determine jurisdiction under the present statutory framework.
Evidence & Defence

Statutory Presumptions and Defence Considerations

Complainant's Case

  • Proof of cheque execution or admission of signature, where relevant.
  • Establishment of statutory presentation and dishonour requirements.
  • Proof of statutory notice and service.
  • Reliance upon presumptions under Sections 118 and 139.
  • Production of transaction records where necessary.
  • Examination of liability and financial capacity if materially disputed.

Accused's Defence

  • Absence of an enforceable debt or liability.
  • Prior repayment, discharge or adjustment of liability.
  • Disputed execution or material documentary inconsistencies.
  • Failure to satisfy statutory notice or limitation requirements.
  • Jurisdiction and corporate liability objections.
  • Rebuttal through cross-examination, existing record or defence evidence.
Rebuttable Presumption: The presumption under Section 139 extends to the existence of an enforceable liability. It is not conclusive. The accused may rebut the presumption by raising a probable defence on the standard of preponderance of probabilities. Personal testimony by the accused is not invariably necessary for rebuttal.
Compensation & Appellate Remedies

Sections 143A and 148 of the NI Act

Section 143A — Interim Compensation

A trial court may, at the applicable procedural stage, direct the drawer to pay interim compensation to the complainant.

  • Maximum amount: twenty per cent of the cheque amount.
  • Exercise of power is discretionary and must be judicially considered.
  • Payment period: sixty days from the order, with an eligible extension not exceeding thirty additional days.
  • Interim compensation is subject to statutory recovery provisions.
  • Upon acquittal, repayment with applicable statutory interest is required in accordance with the provision.

Section 148 — Appellate Deposit

In an appeal by the drawer against conviction under Section 138, the appellate court may direct deposit of an amount under Section 148.

  • The statutory deposit is ordinarily at least twenty per cent of the fine or compensation awarded by the trial court.
  • Exceptional circumstances may justify dispensing with the deposit requirement, subject to judicial reasons.
  • The deposit is additional to interim compensation paid under Section 143A.
  • Payment period ordinarily extends to sixty days, with a permissible additional period not exceeding thirty days.
  • Release and repayment of deposited amounts are governed by statutory conditions.
Important Distinction: Section 143A concerns interim compensation during eligible trial proceedings. Section 148 concerns deposit during an appeal against conviction. Their procedural stages, calculation bases and statutory consequences are different.

Legal Awareness

Frequently Asked Questions

Is every cheque bounce a criminal offence?

No. Criminal liability under Section 138 requires satisfaction of the statutory ingredients, including enforceable liability, timely presentation, statutory notice and failure to make payment within the prescribed period.

What is the punishment under Section 138?

The maximum prescribed punishment is imprisonment extending up to two years, a fine extending up to twice the cheque amount, or both. The actual sentence depends upon judicial determination.

Is a legal notice compulsory before filing a cheque bounce complaint?

Yes. A written statutory demand satisfying Section 138 is an essential precondition for prosecution under that provision.

What happens if payment is made within fifteen days?

If the cheque amount is duly paid within the statutory payment period, the offence under Section 138 does not become complete in respect of that dishonour.

Can a security cheque attract Section 138?

Yes, depending upon the facts. Describing a cheque as security does not automatically exclude Section 138. The existence of an enforceable and matured liability at the relevant point must be examined.

Can a signed blank cheque attract Section 138?

A voluntarily delivered signed blank cheque may attract statutory presumptions. The fact that particulars were completed by another person does not automatically invalidate the cheque. The accused may nevertheless raise a legally sustainable defence based on the circumstances.

Does a stop payment instruction prevent prosecution?

No. Stop payment instructions do not automatically exclude liability under Section 138. The enforceable liability and remaining statutory conditions must be assessed.

Can a dishonoured cheque be presented again?

Repeated presentation during the applicable validity period may be legally permissible. A subsequent dishonour can provide a fresh basis for proceedings if the statutory requirements are independently fulfilled.

What happens if the drawer refuses the statutory notice?

Refusal of notice may constitute legally recognised or deemed service depending upon the address, mode of dispatch, postal endorsement and applicable evidentiary requirements. Service must be determined on the facts.

Is filing a police FIR necessary under Section 138?

No. A prosecution under Section 138 is ordinarily instituted through a written complaint before the competent Magistrate, in accordance with Section 142. Independent offences, if disclosed, must be assessed separately.

Is Section 138 a bailable offence?

Yes. An offence punishable under Section 138 is ordinarily classified as bailable and non-cognizable. Appearance and related procedural requirements remain applicable.

Can every director of a company be made an accused?

No. Liability under Section 141 depends upon the person's role, responsibility at the relevant time, statutory averments and applicable exceptions. Holding a directorship alone does not invariably establish liability.

Is twenty per cent interim compensation mandatory?

No. Section 143A confers discretionary power upon the trial court. Twenty per cent is the maximum permissible interim compensation, not an automatic or mandatory amount.

Is appellate deposit always compulsory under Section 148?

The statutory rule ordinarily requires a deposit of at least twenty per cent of the fine or compensation when a deposit order is made. Exceptional circumstances may justify departing from the ordinary requirement, with appropriate judicial reasons.

Can a cheque bounce case be settled after filing?

Yes. Offences under the Negotiable Instruments Act are compoundable. Settlement and compounding remain subject to applicable legal requirements, consent and judicial consideration.

Can civil recovery and Section 138 proceedings continue simultaneously?

Civil recovery proceedings and criminal cheque dishonour proceedings may coexist where independently maintainable. Payments, compensation and recoveries must be appropriately accounted for to prevent double recovery.

What is the limitation period for filing the complaint?

Section 142 ordinarily requires filing within one calendar month from accrual of the statutory cause of action. Delay in filing may be condoned by the competent court upon sufficient cause being established.

Can a cheque bounce case be filed in Ahmedabad?

A complaint may be instituted before the competent Ahmedabad court where the statutory territorial requirements under Section 142(2), read with other applicable provisions, are satisfied. Residence in Ahmedabad alone may not establish jurisdiction.

Does the law prescribe a six-month trial completion guarantee?

No. Section 143 provides for an endeavour to conclude the trial within six months from filing of the complaint. It does not guarantee disposal within that period in every case.

Is recovery of twice the cheque amount automatic?

No. Twice the cheque amount represents the maximum fine prescribed under Section 138. The actual fine and compensation depend upon judicial determination and applicable law.

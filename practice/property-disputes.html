Ownership, Possession and Protection of Property Rights

Property law governs the acquisition, ownership, transfer, possession, inheritance and enjoyment of immovable property. Disputes may arise from competing ownership claims, defective transactions, family arrangements, contractual obligations or interference with lawful possession.

MNM Legal Associates, based in Chandkheda, Ahmedabad, deals with property-related civil proceedings, partition disputes, title verification, injunction applications, property documentation, land revenue matters and cooperative housing society disputes.

Property matters require careful examination of registered instruments, ownership history, revenue records, succession documents, possession, encumbrances, applicable restrictions and territorial jurisdiction.

The appropriate remedy depends upon the nature of the property, the rights claimed, the documents available, the applicable limitation period and the jurisdiction of the competent court or authority.

Gujarat Property Records

Understanding Land & Property Records

Revenue and registration records perform different legal and administrative functions. They must be examined together with the underlying ownership documents.

Village Form 7/12

Commonly known as the 7/12 extract, this record contains relevant information concerning agricultural land, recorded rights and cultivation details.

  • Survey or block number.
  • Recorded holders and interests.
  • Land area and classification.
  • Relevant agricultural information.

Village Form 8-A

A revenue account record containing information concerning landholdings associated with the relevant account.

  • Khata or account details.
  • Recorded landholdings.
  • Relevant survey numbers.
  • Revenue account information.

Village Form 6

The mutation register records changes proposed or entered in the record of rights arising from transactions, inheritance and other relevant events.

  • Sale-related mutation entries.
  • Inheritance entries.
  • Gift and partition entries.
  • Certification and disputed entries.

Property Card & City Survey Record

Property cards and city survey records provide administrative information concerning properties covered by the relevant city survey system.

  • City survey number.
  • Recorded property holders.
  • Area and relevant interests.
  • Changes in recorded rights.

Index-II & Registered Instruments

Index-II contains indexed particulars of relevant registered transactions. The underlying registered instrument must also be examined.

  • Registration particulars.
  • Parties to the transaction.
  • Property description.
  • Relevant transaction information.

NA Permission & Building Use Permission

Land-use and building permissions concern lawful development and use of property under applicable planning and revenue legislation.

  • Non-agricultural use permission, where required.
  • Development permission.
  • Sanctioned building plans.
  • Building use permission, where applicable.
Important Legal Distinction: Mutation entries, revenue records, property tax receipts and similar administrative records do not, by themselves, create or conclusively establish ownership. A registered instrument must also be examined in light of the transferor's legal title, applicable restrictions and other relevant circumstances.

Legal Awareness

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a mutation entry establish ownership of property?

No. Mutation entries are primarily maintained for revenue and administrative purposes. They do not independently create or extinguish ownership rights. Title must be assessed through legally recognised ownership documents and applicable substantive law.

Does a registered sale deed guarantee clear property title?

Registration is an important legal requirement for specified property transactions. However, registration does not independently guarantee that the transferor had valid and transferable title. Previous ownership, encumbrances and applicable restrictions must also be examined.

Can a daughter claim a share in ancestral property?

Under Section 6 of the Hindu Succession Act, as amended in 2005, a daughter is a coparcener by birth in a Mitakshara Hindu joint family in the same manner as a son. The actual entitlement depends upon the nature of the property and applicable statutory provisions, including protected prior transactions.

Is every property inherited from a father ancestral property?

No. Property inherited from a parent is not automatically coparcenary or ancestral property in the technical legal sense. Its legal character depends upon the manner of acquisition, devolution and applicable succession law.

Can one co-owner sell jointly owned property without consent?

A co-owner may generally transfer their own transferable interest, subject to applicable restrictions. A co-owner cannot ordinarily convey the interests of other co-owners without lawful authority. Special considerations may apply to an undivided family dwelling house.

Can a sale deed be cancelled through a civil court?

An eligible person may seek cancellation of a void or voidable written instrument under Section 31 of the Specific Relief Act, subject to statutory requirements. Depending upon the claimant's relationship to the instrument, declaratory or other consequential relief may be appropriate.

Can a court stop the sale of disputed property?

A competent court may grant a temporary injunction in appropriate circumstances. Such relief generally requires examination of a prima facie case, balance of convenience and irreparable injury. The grant of an injunction is not automatic.

What is the legal effect of selling property during pending litigation?

The doctrine of lis pendens under Section 52 of the Transfer of Property Act may apply where rights in immovable property are directly and specifically in question in pending proceedings. Such a transfer is not automatically void but may remain subject to the outcome of the litigation, applicable local amendments and statutory requirements.

What is the limitation period for a property dispute?

There is no single limitation period applicable to every property dispute. For example, Articles 54, 58 and 59 of the Limitation Act generally prescribe three-year periods for specified reliefs, with different starting points. Article 65 generally prescribes twelve years for possession based on title, beginning when the defendant's possession becomes adverse to the plaintiff.

Does twelve years of possession automatically create ownership?

No. Mere long possession is not sufficient to establish adverse possession. The claimant must establish the required legal ingredients, including possession adverse to the true owner's title for the applicable statutory period. Permissive occupation is not ordinarily adverse possession.

What remedy is available against unlawful dispossession?

Section 6 of the Specific Relief Act provides a summary remedy in specified cases of dispossession without consent otherwise than in due course of law. Such a suit must be brought within six months of dispossession, subject to statutory restrictions. Separate title-based remedies may also be available.

Can a housing society recover unpaid maintenance charges?

A cooperative housing society may pursue recovery of lawfully imposed maintenance charges through remedies available under applicable legislation and its registered bye-laws. The appropriate forum and procedure depend upon the nature of the dispute and statutory requirements.

Does a power of attorney transfer ownership of property?

A power of attorney ordinarily authorises another person to act on behalf of the principal. It is not by itself a conveyance of ownership. A legally authorised attorney may execute a valid conveyance on behalf of the owner, subject to applicable legal requirements.

Can a buyer claim ownership merely on the basis of an agreement to sell?

Section 54 of the Transfer of Property Act provides that a contract for sale does not, by itself, create any interest in or charge on the property. Contractual and other statutory remedies may nevertheless be available in appropriate circumstances.

Can anyone purchase agricultural land in Gujarat?

Agricultural land transfers in Gujarat may be subject to restrictions concerning agriculturist status, land tenure, permissions and statutory exceptions. Eligibility must be examined under the legislation applicable to the particular land and proposed transaction.

What should be verified before purchasing a flat or plot?

Relevant matters include ownership history, registered instruments, encumbrances, pending litigation, applicable land-use restrictions, sanctioned plans, building permissions, society records and the legal authority of the proposed seller.

Can a revenue authority finally decide disputed civil ownership?

Revenue authorities generally determine matters within their statutory revenue jurisdiction. Disputed questions of substantive civil title ordinarily require adjudication by a competent civil court, unless a special statute confers jurisdiction upon another forum.

Which court has jurisdiction over property disputes in Ahmedabad?

Territorial jurisdiction in suits concerning immovable property is generally determined with reference to the location of the property, subject to Section 16 CPC and other applicable provisions. Pecuniary jurisdiction, valuation and special statutory jurisdiction must also be considered.
